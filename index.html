The problem

Clear glass is invisible — and that's dangerous.

Frameless glass doors and partitions look modern and open, but under bright or even lighting they can become almost impossible to see. People walk straight into glass they didn't know was there.

A useful UK reference

Use two visual zones on large areas of glazing.

850–1000 mm
above finished floor level

1400–1600 mm
above finished floor level

UK guidance describes manifestation as broken or solid lines, patterns or company logos that contrast visually with the background, from both directions and in different lighting conditions.

UK GUIDANCE04
Recommendation
Suggested direction for Divar

One continuous red stripe, left to right.

A single, solid stripe running the full width of the glass, left to right, at the primary eye-level band — in Divar's own Primary red.

  • Uses Divar's own brand red — the mark becomes brand presence, not a safety afterthought.
  • A full, unbroken stripe reads as intentional design, not a compliance sticker.
  • High contrast in every lighting condition, satisfying the visibility requirement outright.
  • One straightforward cut of vinyl — fast, low-cost, easy to reproduce on every office glass door.

DESIGN MOCKUP06
Recommended specification

For a modern corporate office

  • Use appropriate safety glazing where human impact is foreseeable.
  • Apply durable, high-contrast manifestation to large transparent surfaces.
  • Use the two UK reference height zones where applicable.
  • Clearly differentiate doors from adjacent fixed glazing.
  • For Divar: a single full-width red stripe, left to right, in the brand's Primary red.
  • Check the final design against the local building code and glazing specification.
